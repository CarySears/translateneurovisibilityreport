How We Score Your Digital Presence

Your overall visibility score is a weighted composite of 10 audit categories, each measuring a different dimension of your online presence. The weights reflect what actually drives local visibility, trust and lead generation - not vanity metrics.

The part worth knowing up front: the numbers are measurements, not opinions. Every category score is produced by a fixed formula reading data fetched from your website, your Google Business Profile, Google's own performance tooling, a live scan of business directories, and third-party search indexes. An AI model writes the explanations you read underneath them; it does not choose the scores. Where a score could not be measured on this run, this page says so by name.

How the Overall Score Is Calculated

Each category receives a raw score from 0 to 100 based on audit findings. Each raw score is then multiplied by the category weight and summed to produce the overall visibility score.

Worked example. Technical SEO carries a weight of 20%. A category score of 45 therefore contributes 45 × 0.20 = 9.00 points to the overall figure. The same arithmetic runs for all ten categories and the results are added together. There is no rounding, curve or adjustment applied afterwards - the overall score is exactly the sum of its parts.

The checks and balances

Five rules that stop a score from flattering a site - or from punishing one unfairly

A failed check is not a failed site

If we request your sitemap and the request times out, that tells us nothing about whether the sitemap exists. Those checks are marked not determinable and removed from the calculation entirely, rather than counted as a zero. A slow server on the day we looked cannot cost you points.

A check that cannot fail is not a check

Every check has to have a way to score zero, or it is decoration. We test whether http:// genuinely redirects to https:// rather than just reading the address we were given. We parse your robots.txt rather than only checking that the file exists - a robots.txt that blocks every search engine scores worse than having none at all. We check whether your sitemap is real XML, because plenty of servers answer that address with an ordinary "page not found" page.

The score cannot contradict the findings

If a category reports a critical issue, its score is capped at 60; a high-severity issue caps it at 80. A report cannot show you a near-perfect number sitting directly above a list of serious problems - if the findings are real, the score reflects them.

Nothing scores a perfect 100 from one page

Technical SEO tops out at 92 because we inspect your homepage, and a homepage cannot prove that a whole site is clean - duplicate titles across templates, crawl depth, redirect chains and orphaned pages are not visible from it. The remaining points are reserved for evidence we do not yet collect. We would rather leave them unclaimed than award them for something we did not look at.

One website, one measurement

Businesses with several locations share one website. Those locations are measured once and the result is shared between them, so two branches of the same company cannot receive different scores for the same site.

Check our work

Because the measurements are observations rather than opinions, you can reproduce most of them yourself in a browser in about five minutes. If any of these disagree with what this report says, tell us - a measurement that cannot be checked is not worth reporting.

  • Type your domain with http:// and watch whether the address bar switches to https://.
  • Open yourdomain.com/robots.txt and yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml directly.
  • View the page source and search for noindex, canonical, and og:image.
  • Count the <h1> tags in that source - there should be exactly one.
  • Run your homepage through Google PageSpeed Insights and compare the Core Web Vitals to the ones in this report.
  • Open your Google Business Profile and compare the review count, rating, categories and hours.

How Scores Drive Recommendations

Categories with higher weights that score poorly are elevated into Priority 1 (fix-now) actions. The recommended package is selected based on the overall maturity level, the number of critical and high-severity issues, and the implementation scope required.

Because the scores are measurements, the priorities follow from them rather than the other way round. If you fix a measured problem and we re-run the audit, the number moves - and you can verify the change yourself using the checks above.
