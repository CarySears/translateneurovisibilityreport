What the findings tell us

Translate Neuroscience presents recognizable services around memory assessment, cognitive rehabilitation, brain-health coaching, and dementia caregiver support. The scans also surfaced questions about how readily patients and families can discover those services, understand next steps, and contact the practice. These are observations to review together, not a conclusion that every flagged item requires correction.

Search discovery

Local ranking scans did not identify the Google Business Profile in the tracked Los Angeles grid for the two tested queries. The selected AI recommendation prompts also did not surface the brand. Both findings are limited to the locations, questions, tools, and ranking ranges tested.

Website accessibility and clarity

Site scans reported mobile loading and several technical/content-access issues. Some automated tools could identify the business phone number, while others could not confirm an enquiry or consultation-booking route. Those user journeys warrant hands-on verification.

Business identity

Website and LinkedIn branding use “Translate Neuroscience,” while the Google listing uses “Translate Neuro.” LinkedIn also displayed a .co domain, versus the active .com website. These differences should be checked for intent and consistency rather than automatically classified as errors.

Market strategy

Los Angeles was chosen for initial testing because of Collin’s professional connection to the area and USC. That testing choice does not establish the business’s physical location or determine whether a Los Angeles-first or California-wide strategy is best.

Evidence: the existing Intel audit, additional October 20 diagnostic scan, shared Google Maps and heatmap screenshots, and service descriptions on the client website. Automated scans may disagree or miss content rendered dynamically.

AI visibility · Context from multiple checks

AI recommendations and AI-readiness are different measurements

Recommendation visibility: In the selected test responses, Translate Neuro/Translate Neuroscience was not named. This is evidence about those specific prompts, not proof that the practice never appears in ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, or Google AI results.

Technical readiness: A separate scan reported that common AI-related crawlers were not blocked by the checked robots directives, and that an llms.txt file was present. It also flagged incomplete machine-readable business details, JavaScript-dependent content, and a missing social-sharing preview image. These are separate from actual recommendation frequency. An llms.txt file by itself does not guarantee inclusion in AI answers.

What to verify: Review the site's rendered HTML and structured data for business name, phone, services, correct locations or service areas, and clear ways to enquire or book. Confirm the relevant AI crawlers can obtain important service content without depending on unsupported rendering assumptions.

Language and search intent: The website offers Traditional Chinese content, and Collin reports a substantial Chinese patient population. A later test could evaluate Chinese-language discovery, after confirming which spoken languages are available for patient care. Traditional Chinese script alone does not establish Mandarin- or Cantonese-language clinical services.

The initial AI probes included questions about memory evaluations, dementia coordination, and language-related patient needs. The scanned queries are only a sample; repeatability and prompt wording affect results.
